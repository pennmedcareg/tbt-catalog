Rules that apply to every site

STORE and SEER first-course principles

First course is the plan, not the calendar

Everything in the documented initial treatment plan counts, even if it starts months later (endocrine therapy, adjuvant IO, maintenance BCG). With no documented plan, use the treatment given before disease progression or the STORE time window.

Recommended but not given

If a treatment was recommended and the patient declined, code it as refused rather than "not given." If it was not part of the plan, use the "not recommended" reason. Medical oncology and radiation consult notes are the usual proof.

Neoadjuvant fields

When systemic therapy or radiation comes before surgery, complete Neoadjuvant Therapy, Clinical Response, and Treatment Effect, use yp staging for the post-therapy pathologic stage, and check the Systemic/Surgery and RX Summ–Surg/Rad Seq fields.

Drug categories

Monoclonal antibodies such as trastuzumab and most targeted pills are coded as chemotherapy. Checkpoint inhibitors and BCG are immunotherapy. Anti-androgens and aromatase inhibitors are hormone therapy. Look up anything unfamiliar in SEER*Rx before coding.

Text that supports every code

Every coded field should be traceable to a line of text: date, facility or source, and the finding. Lead each entry with the date, use NAACCR-approved abbreviations, and write "N/A" or "none per [source]" rather than leaving a field blank so reviewers know you looked.

Document the why

When treatment differs from the expected pathway, put the reason in Remarks: refusal, comorbidity, clinical trial, patient preference, or care at another facility. That is what the cancer committee and quality measure reviews need.
